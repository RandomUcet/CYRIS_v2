# CYRIS v2.4.5 — Medical team visibility, transfer and working handoff form

## Medical correctness
- New medical records without a specified owning team are automatically assigned to the operator's **single active medical-capable unit** in the exercise. This means colleagues on the receiving medical team can actually find records created in their medical workspace. If there is no unambiguous medical unit (e.g., an administrator, multiple medical memberships), the safe owner-only default is retained; no team is guessed.
- Medical team reassignment synchronizes the `medical_encounters.assigned_unit_id` with `resources.owning_unit_id`, the field used by the central resource-access service. Team reassignment requires `medical.transfer` in addition to `medical.update`, and the receiving unit must be an active medical-capable team in the same exercise.
- Cross-team handoff atomically updates clinical location, receiving unit, assigned medical operator, resource ownership, audit and search outbox. The former private owner and assignee no longer retain access just because they created or previously held the record; existing immutable audit history remains.
- A concurrent reassignment or handoff cannot silently overwrite a newer team ownership state.
- The transferring operator receives an accurate one-time success response after the authorized handoff even when subsequent GET requests are no longer permitted for the original team.

## Operator UX
- The previously incomplete medical handoff dialog now includes the **required destination location selector**, an optional receiving medical team selector, and retains the entered handoff notes.
- Options load from the exercise registry and team directory, filtering out archived/non-medical units. Fetch errors and empty destination registries are clearly displayed. Duplicate submissions are blocked; success is acknowledged.
- Location choices are validated server-side against the same event. Server-side restrictions apply even if a caller forges form values.

## Regression / scope
- Isolated PostgreSQL tests verify unique vs ambiguous medical-unit selection, medical ACLs before/after team ownership changes, old-owner revocation, successful handoff acknowledgement and full rollback on simulated outbox failure.
- Web source regressions cover a usable destination input, receiver selection, notes, loading/error states and reuse of the existing registry API method.
- No schema migration, destructive deletion, new dependency or policy change for other domains.
- Exercise/operator browser acceptance of real team permissions, print outputs and handoff must still be performed before deploying to a live exercise.
